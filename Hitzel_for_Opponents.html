Commander · mono-green · deck-out combo

Hitzel's Sequence

Nobody takes damage. Nobody attacks. You lose because your library runs out and theirs doesn't. Here is exactly how that happens, which cards genuinely stop it, and which ones only look like they do.

The whole thing in one paragraph

They assemble unlimited mana and a way to untap one specific land over and over. That land makes every player draw a card and then discard a card. Repeat it enough times and everyone's library empties at the same rate — except they have a creature that scoops their own discard pile back into their library, and you don't. They refill; you hit zero; you lose the next time you're asked to draw.

The engine is three cards, and one of them is the win

Geier Reach Sanitarium — legendary land {T}: Add {C}. {2}, {T}: Each player draws a card, then discards a card.

That second ability is the entire win condition. Each activation costs one card from every library at the table, including theirs. It is not optional for you and it cannot be responded to profitably — there will be a great many activations.

Two things turn one activation into arbitrarily many:

A repeatable untap for the Sanitarium

Any one of these does it. They are interchangeable, and the deck likely plays several — so removing one is rarely enough. Note that five of the six are creatures (marked): that is what makes the shut-off effects below so much better than spot removal.

  • Deserted Temple
  • Argothian Elder
  • Hope Tender
  • Magus of the Candelabra
  • Ley Weaver
  • Woodcaller Automaton

Unlimited green mana

From whichever of the deck's mana loops it assembled — most often Ashaya, Soul of the Wild with a Ranger and a large mana creature, or a Temur Sabertooth bounce loop.

Note what is not in that list: Endurance. Endurance does not mill you and is not part of the kill. It is their life support.

Three versions, three shopping lists

The deck likely has access to three variants. They share the Sanitarium, unlimited mana, an untapper and Endurance; what differs is how Endurance gets recast every iteration, and that is the part you can attack. Green-bordered pieces are creatures.

A

Temur — the short one

  • Temur Sabertooth

Cast Endurance, hold its trigger on the stack, bounce it to hand with Sabertooth, activate the Sanitarium, untap, repeat. Fewest moving parts and the version you will meet most often.

Aim at: Sabertooth. It is the only recursion piece, and it is a creature.

B

Kogla — the one that kills its own Endurance

  • Kogla, the Titan Ape
  • Eternal Witness
  • Beast Within
  • Legolas's Quick Reflexes

Endurance is destroyed on purpose while its trigger is still on the stack, so Eternal Witness can return it from the graveyard; Kogla bounces the Witness to do it again. This is why "kill the Endurance in response" is not just useless but actively helpful to them.

Aim at: Eternal Witness, or Kogla. Two creatures, both essential, neither replaceable by the other.

C

Ashaya — the one with the extra tutor

  • Ashaya, Soul of the Wild
  • Quirion Ranger
  • Scryb Ranger
  • Duskwatch Recruiter
  • Legolas's Quick Reflexes

A Ranger bounces Endurance by returning a Forest; the line alternates two Endurance triggers so that the one which resolves shuffles Endurance and the Ranger back into the library — where Duskwatch Recruiter immediately digs them out again, for free, as many times as needed.

Aim at: Duskwatch Recruiter. It is a two-mana 2/2 doing the work of a tutor engine, it is easy to leave alone because it looks like a value creature, and without it this variant has no way to re-find the pieces it keeps burying.

The asymmetry is the whole combo

Endurance — {1}{G}{G} · flash · 3/4 When this creature enters, up to one target player puts all the cards from their graveyard on the bottom of their library in a random order.

They target themselves. Every card they discarded to the Sanitarium goes back under their library. Because it has flash, they can hold it until their own library is nearly gone and then cast it as a refuel.

It never touches your graveyard. That single targeting restriction is the reason a symmetrical effect kills only you.

Notice what this makes their library: not a resource that depletes, but a closed loop. An activation moves one of their cards from library to graveyard; Endurance moves them all back. Nothing is spent. If they can recast Endurance freely — and the bounce versions do it once per activation — the size of their library stops mattering at all. Yours, meanwhile, only goes one way.

The cards this deck is afraid of

Note: almost none of them interact with the Sanitarium at all. They turn off creatures, because everything except the win condition itself is a creature.

Turns the engine off

Torpor Orb · Hushwing Gryff · Hushbringer · Tocatli Honor Guard

These are probably the best answers in the game to this deck. Endurance's refuel is an enters-the-battlefield trigger. Switch those off and Endurance is a 3/4 with reach — the loop still mills everyone, including them, and their library is likely the smaller one. They don't just fail to win; they can't safely start.

The same effect blanks Eternal Witness, Kogla, Woodcaller Automaton, Scrapshooter and Disciple of Freyalise — most of the deck's other win conditions run on ETB triggers too.

Cost: Torpor Orb is an artifact into Force of Vigor and Bane of Progress; the three creature versions dodge that but can be fought. All four are symmetrical — check your own deck first.

Cursed Totem · Linvala, Keeper of Silence

Creatures' activated abilities stop working. That is four of the six untappers, plus Temur Sabertooth, both Rangers, Duskwatch Recruiter, Elvish Reclaimer and likely every mana dork in the deck. What would survive is Deserted Temple — a land, and one they now have no mana to use.

Linvala only hits your opponents, so prefer her if your own deck taps creatures for anything. Cursed Totem is symmetrical but harder to remove out of green.

Humility

Everything becomes a 1/1 with no abilities, so nothing on the list above matters. Endurance entering under Humility has no trigger to put on the stack at all, and Ashaya stops turning creatures into lands.

Hard to cast at a green table, and enchantment removal is exactly what mono-green has. Expect it to eat a card, not to lock the game.

Turns their loop into their loss

Orcish Bowmasters

The single most useful card here, because it is the only one that still works after the loop starts. It has flash, and the Sanitarium makes every player draw — so every activation makes an opponent draw outside their draw step, and every one of those pings for 1, aimed wherever you like. Milling out a Commander library takes tens of activations. They cannot pay that in life.

It answers the deck's other draw-based kills the same way: the Mikokoro loop and the Disciple of Freyalise draw loop both work by drawing cards.

Hold it. Played on curve it is a two-mana value creature they will kill on sight; held up, it is the card that makes the combo unprofitable.

Mayhem Devil

Not an answer to the Sanitarium line, which never sacrifices anything — but the deck's Disciple of Freyalise loop sacrifices a creature every iteration, and Devil converts that into unbounded damage that you point. Keep it in mind as coverage for the rest of the deck rather than for this sequence.

Attacks the setup, not the loop

Opposition Agent

This is the card that makes land destruction stick. Several cards likely in the deck can search up the Sanitarium, so blowing it up on its own is a delay; while Opposition Agent is out, all seven find nothing and you take the card instead. Flash, so it can arrive in response to the tutor.

Jeska, Thrice Reborn

Repeatable damage from a permanent mono-green struggles to remove. It won't stop a loop that is already running, but across several turns it can pick off whichever creature is currently load-bearing — the untapper, the Sabertooth, the Duskwatch — and force them to re-find each one.

Drannith Magistrate

Locks the commander out of the command zone, so once Yeva is dealt with she stays dealt with. Honest about what it isn't: every piece of the sequence is cast from hand, so this taxes the rebuild rather than stopping the line.

Five things that feel like answers and aren't

Kill the Endurance in response.

Removing the creature does nothing to the trigger. Once that enters-the-battlefield ability is on the stack it exists independently of the creature that made it, and it will still recycle their graveyard. Variant B deliberately destroys its own Endurance while the trigger is waiting, because that puts it in the graveyard where Eternal Witness can return it for another lap.

Counter the Endurance.

Worth doing, but budget for it not being the end. Eternal Witness returns it from the graveyard, and Kogla, the Titan Ape or Temur Sabertooth bounce the Witness to hand to do it again. Expect to need more than one answer.

Gain life, fog, prevent the damage.

There is no damage anywhere in this line, and no combat. Life total is irrelevant — you die to an empty library.

Exile their graveyard and they have nothing to recycle.

Only if it is a static effect. A one-shot exile removes cards from a pool that does not need to be big — see the section below. This one catches people out precisely because it feels like the clean answer.

Hold up an answer and act mid-loop.

Usually you won't get the chance. The written sequences hold priority through each cycle specifically to avoid handing you a window between activations. Assume that once the loop is genuinely running, it finishes. Orcish Bowmasters is the exception, and it is an exception because it never needs priority.

Where you can actually act

In time order. The earlier ones matter far more than the later ones.

Before

Attack the mana, not the mill

Every part of this needs unlimited mana. The Sanitarium loop is only the payoff bolted onto whichever infinite-mana engine the deck found. Killing the mana creature — the Ashaya, the Ranger, the large dork — is the real answer, and it is the one that also stops every other win the deck has.

Before

Killing the land buys a turn, not the game

Geier Reach Sanitarium is legendary, so a second copy does them no good, and Strip Mine, Ghost Quarter and Field of Ruin all answer it. Do it — but know what you are buying.

Several cards likely in the deck can go and get it again: Expedition Map, Crop Rotation, Sylvan Scrying, Urza's Cave, Archdruid's Charm, Sowing Mycospawn and Elvish Reclaimer. Four of those put it straight onto the battlefield, and two — Crop Rotation and Archdruid's Charm — are instants, so a fetch in response to your Strip Mine can mean you don't even get the turn.

And the Sanitarium is not the only symmetrical draw land here. Mikokoro, Center of the Sea does the same job for the same {2}, has its own documented loop, and is equally legendary. Treat land destruction as a tempo play that costs them a card and a tutor — genuinely worth it, but not a removal spell for the win condition.

Any time

Deny the refuel — but only a static effect does it

Graveyard hate is the right instinct and the wrong card. To see why, count what an activation actually does to them: one card leaves the library, one card enters the graveyard. Nothing is destroyed. Endurance moves the whole pile back. Their library and graveyard together are a closed pool, and the loop just cycles cards around it.

That is why one-shot exile fails. In the bounce versions they recast Endurance every single activation — cast it, bounce it with Temur Sabertooth while the trigger is on the stack, activate the Sanitarium, repeat. Their library never drops more than a card or two below where the refuel puts it back. Exiling the graveyard once takes away a pool they did not need to be large; a handful of cards is enough to run the loop indefinitely.

What does work is a replacement effect that never lets the graveyard fill: Rest in Peace, Leyline of the Void, Planar Void. Now each discard is exiled instead, Endurance's trigger finds nothing every time, and their library falls by one per activation with no way back. Everyone drains at the same rate — and their library is smaller than yours, because they have likely been drawing and tutoring extra cards.

Be precise about what that wins you, though: it makes the mill line unprofitable, so they simply won't run it. It does not win the game. This deck has a long list of other ways to close, most of which don't care about graveyards at all. And they can answer an enchantment — this is mono-green with Force of Vigor and Bane of Progress. It buys a turn and forces them to spend a card. That is all, and it is still worth doing.

On the trigger

One-shot exile, only against the no-engine version

There is a version of this line with no bounce engine at all: Endurance is simply cast from hand once, as a single large refuel, roughly doubling their remaining activations to library-plus-graveyard. Against that version, exiling the graveyard in response to the trigger genuinely matters — it halves the budget, which can put the largest library at the table out of reach.

The catch is that you usually cannot tell which version you are facing until the bounce engine shows up. Treat instant-speed exile as a reasonable gamble, not the answer.

Too late

Once it is assembled, it is over

Countering one activation does nothing against an unbounded number of them. If unlimited mana, the Sanitarium and a repeatable untapper are all on the battlefield at once with Endurance available, the game is decided. Spend your interaction before that point — the one card that still helps you here is Orcish Bowmasters, because it charges them per activation whether they give you priority or not.

Spotting it early

The tell is Geier Reach Sanitarium in a mono-green deck. Green has almost no reason to play a colourless legendary land whose ability helps everyone draw. If it hits the battlefield, it is there to win the game. Mikokoro, Center of the Sea is the same tell wearing a different hat.

Treat it as urgent when you also see an untapper — Deserted Temple, Hope Tender, Argothian Elder — or any of the deck's mana loops starting to assemble. The gap between "that looks fine" and "the loop is running" is usually one turn.

The quieter tell is Duskwatch Recruiter. It reads as a small value creature and it is a tutor engine; against variant C it is the piece holding the loop together, and it is the one people are less likely to spend removal on.
